Meta tells you how many people started a conversation. It can't tell you which of them became a sale. Ads closes that gap: it takes the leads that arrived through your Click-to-WhatsApp ads and follows them all the way to won or lost.

## The table

One row per ad, with leads, qualified, % qualified, won and value. If you connect your Meta account you also get spend, budget and cost per qualified lead, and you can group by campaign or ad set.

Three things about those numbers are worth knowing before you draw conclusions:

- **"Qualified" is strict.** It counts only opportunities the *agent* qualified. Anything a rep pushed into the pipeline by hand doesn't count — otherwise the number would measure your team's optimism instead of the ad's quality.
- **The current period's numbers keep going up.** A lead that arrived today may qualify tomorrow, and when it does it counts back to the day it arrived. That's why comparing a half-finished month against a completed one always flatters the completed one.
- **Without a connected Meta account you still get the leads.** Those come from your own data. What disappears is the money: spend, budget and cost per qualified.

<Note>
**"No ad" and "From an ad (we don't know which)" are not the same thing**, and mixing them up is the easiest mistake here. The first is genuinely organic — referrals, someone who had your number saved. The second did come from your ads; the ad's identifier just didn't arrive. Only the first sits outside the table.
</Note>

This is the part that changes results rather than just reporting them.

With it off, Meta only knows that someone started a conversation. So it optimizes for that: it goes and finds you more people who start conversations — which means more curious browsers.

Switch it on and Trama tells Meta when a lead **qualifies, gets quoted or buys**. Meta stops looking for people who chat and starts looking for people who look like the ones who buy from you.

**The only thing shared is the phone number, turned into an unreadable code first.** Neither Meta nor anyone else can get the number back out of it. The name and the conversation never leave.

It ships off by default, on purpose: it's your customers' data and turning it on should be your decision, not ours.

<Warning>
**The sale almost never makes it in time, and that's not a bug.** Meta only accepts news of what happened within 7 days of the click, and a trip usually closes weeks after the first message. Qualification and quoting do arrive within the window — those are the ones that will actually train your campaigns.
</Warning>

To measure you need a connected WhatsApp number: the events are reported against the number the conversation came in on.
