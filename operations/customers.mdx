## What we know

This is the best part of the section, and it's easy to miss. Trama reads the conversations and writes down what it learned as separate facts: *"Travels with her teenage daughter"*, *"Compares three or four agencies before closing"*, *"Prefers voice notes over long texts"*, *"Cares more about the pool than the hotel's star rating"*.

They're not a summary paragraph — each one stands alone, so you can delete the one that's wrong without losing the rest. You can add your own too, tagged by category.

Read together, they're a sales briefing. Someone who compares four agencies and prefers voice notes needs a different follow-up than someone who books on the spot.

## Tags

  <img className="hidden dark:block" src="/images/clientes/tags-dark.png" alt="Tag filter applied to the customer list" />

The agent writes tags as it talks: occasion (`honeymoon`, `anniversary`), trip type (`beach`, `snow`), destinations of interest (`caribbean`), and preferences (`all-inclusive`, `low-cost`). Only what the customer said or what's clearly implied.

They're normalized on the way in — lowercase, no accents, hyphens instead of spaces — which is why you see `15th-birthday` and not `15th Birthday`. That's what makes them work as filters.

Click any tag above the table to filter by it. You can also edit tags by hand on the customer's record, and add ones the agent would never infer.

## The record

Personal details, location, channels the customer wrote from, the history of every opportunity they've had, and where the lead originally came from.

Also **who owns them**, assigned automatically to the rep who takes their first opportunity. It isn't just a label: it's what decides who can see this customer — it's explained in [Visibility](/team/visibility).

## When the same person appears twice

It happens constantly: they write from WhatsApp, then fill in the web form, then a colleague loads them by hand.

Trama merges them on its own **only when there's a shared verifiable identifier** — the same phone, the same email, the same verified identity. **Never by name**: too many real, different people share one.

When it isn't sure, it doesn't decide. It proposes: a "Possible duplicates" list where you compare both records side by side and choose. You can also merge two by hand from the record itself.

<Note>
Merging loses nothing and deletes nothing. Conversations, opportunities and history all move to the surviving record, the tags of both are combined rather than replaced, and you can **undo it for 30 days**.
</Note>
